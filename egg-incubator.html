Dino egg incubator

Many processes need a surface held at a prescribed temperature: curing composites, activating adhesives on shoe soles, drying coatings or forming plastic sheets. Automating it is hard, because the heat spreads through the material and escapes to the air, so the source has to keep moving and adjusting its power; try it yourself. Our ergodic heat coverage control (EHCC) does it without a thermal model of the material, and Show solution lets it incubate the egg.

Reaching the target on average is not enough: the bracket on the error bar is the spread of the error, which grows when parts of the shell are warmer than others, so the whole egg has to be equally warm. Keep the average and the spread inside the band for 30 seconds in Easy or 40 seconds in Hard.

Move the pointer over the egg to aim the source and press to heat. Drag the Power slider to the right, or scroll up over the egg, for more power. Keyboard: arrow keys aim, Space heats, W increases power, S decreases it, and P pauses.

  • Signed bandBoth the average error and its spread must stay between −2 °C and +2 °C. Leaving the band resets the count.
  • Cold frontIn Hard mode, the outside temperature drops for a few seconds and the egg loses heat faster.
  • SnowflakeA ring marks where it will land; on impact it cools that part of the shell.

Temperature-dependent sex determination: in crocodilians and many turtles, the incubation temperature sets the sex of the hatchling. In the American alligator, cooler nests produce females and warmer nests males; in many turtles the relation is reversed. Whether non-avian dinosaurs had this mechanism is not known (birds, the living dinosaurs, have chromosomal sex determination). In this game, 25 °C hatches a female and 30 °C a male.

The temperature field follows the heat equation of the EHCC problem setup, ∂T/∂t = α∇²T + λ(q_in − h(T − T_amb)), on the egg with insulated edges. Higher Power concentrates more heat into a smaller area; lower Power spreads gentler heat over a wider area. The source cannot cool: only diffusion and losses to the air lower the temperature.
